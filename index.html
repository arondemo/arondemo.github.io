A. Multiple choice

Select one answer per question. 1 mark each.

1. A district gives every village the same borrowing rights, but some villages are much farther from the nearest library. Which change best improves equity without changing those rights?
2. A committee assumes all students from one region are poor speakers. It then refuses to audition anyone from that region. Which interpretation best distinguishes the two actions?
3. Two youth groups disagree about using a hall on Saturday. Which development is the clearest sign that the conflict has spread beyond the original booking?
4. A neutral librarian helps two clubs discuss a timetable. Each club then gives up one of its preferred time slots to reach a deal. Which statement is most accurate?
5. A survey finds that household earnings have risen, but many of the same households still lack safe water and their children often miss school. Which conclusion is best supported?
6. After two groups agree to stop fighting, UN monitors check whether both sides are following the agreement. Which area of UN work is most directly involved?
7. Communities that fish along the same river disagree about access. The two countries the river passes through now disagree over a fishing agreement. At what level is the dispute between their governments?
8. A school wants to serve its own community and think about how its spending affects people in other countries. Which plan best does both?

E. Essay

Write about 160 to 220 words. 8 marks.

A district council in Indonesia has one grant for a youth arts programme. Option A is one large festival in the town centre. Option B funds smaller workshops in several villages. Some villages are far from town, and young people speak different languages at home. The council wants them to learn from one another without giving up their traditions.

  • Weigh one strength and one limitation of each option.
  • Propose two practical steps within the chosen option: one to remove a barrier and one to help young people from different villages work together.
  • End with a justified judgement about what the council should fund and how it could support unity in diversity.
