A. Reading [28 marks]

Text 1: One Minute on Air

  1. Sinta was early for the school radio broadcast. She had practised introducing the music show until she could say every word without looking down. In her pocket was a folded card with the interview questions she had written in case the guest arrived early.
  2. At three minutes to twelve, Damar leaned over the desk. “The guest is still on the bus,” he said. Sinta looked at the timetable taped next to the mixer: the interview was meant to come before the first song. She picked up her script, then set it down upside down without noticing.
  3. Through the studio window, Sinta could see students waiting by the speaker in the courtyard. Someone waved. The silence was a lid on the room. Damar pointed at the music controls, but she knew the computer took almost a minute to load a song after it had been switched on.
  4. She made a tentative move towards the microphone. Her hand hovered over the switch, then drew back. From the case beside the desk came the sound of Damar's phone vibrating. He read the message but did not look up.
  5. Sinta unfolded her card. The final question was about a noise in the neighbourhood that the guest would miss if she ever moved away. Sinta thought of the bell on the bread seller's bicycle. She put the card next to the microphone, took a slow breath and decided she could fill the opening minute herself. Then she reached for the switch again.

Text 2: Where Should the Courtyard Get Shade?

  1. What the team measured. Two groups in the school science club compared shaded and unshaded parts of the courtyard. One group worked at lunchtime on the tiled path. The other worked after lessons on the darker playground surface. Both groups placed identical thermometers at the same height and let them settle before reading the air temperature.
  2. What the numbers showed. In both sets of measurements, shaded spots were cooler than nearby unshaded spots. Some committee members called this a clear case for building shade over the playground first. However, the groups recorded different temperatures even in shade, and the committee could not tell whether the time of day, the surface below, or something else explained the difference.
  3. What we still need to know. The club called its results provisional. Its members suggested taking more readings at the same time on both surfaces before recommending a location. They also proposed counting how many students use each area on ordinary school days. The cooler spot is useful only if students can reach it and want to spend time there.
  4. As the club's report put it, “The first measurements tell us that shade is worth looking at. They don't yet tell us where to build it.” The committee will use the next set of results before making a final decision.

This investigation is fictional and was written for this practice test.
